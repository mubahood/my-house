# The House: Architectural Description

A two storey family house planned around a **double height living hall**. The ground floor holds the shared living spaces, the kitchen and the bedroom wing. The upper floor holds the private suite: master bedroom, en suite bathroom, home office and an open lounge. An **L shaped gallery** links them, overlooks the living hall and opens onto the front balcony.

---

## 1. The house at a glance

| Level | Spaces |
|---|---|
| Outside, front | Front veranda |
| Ground floor | Double height living hall, open plan dining area, kitchen, staircase, bedroom corridor, Bedroom 2, Bedroom 3, guest bedroom, self contained Room 4 |
| Upper floor | Open landing lounge, L shaped gallery, master bedroom, en suite bathroom, home office, front balcony |

---

## 2. Ground floor

### 2.1 Double height living hall
- The largest space in the house: a floor area the size of **four rooms joined into one**.
- It has **no floor above it**. The ceiling rises the full height of the house, up to the roof.
- It is the main sitting room and the heart of the plan. Every other space is reached from here.
- The upper floor gallery looks down into it from above (see 3.2).

### 2.2 Open plan dining area
- Sits at the front of the house, **open to the living hall**, with no wall between them.
- Living and dining read as one continuous space, with the dining zone nearer the front.

### 2.3 Entrance steps and front veranda
- The house stands on a **raised plinth**, so the ground floor sits above the yard.
- A broad flight of **entrance steps**, about five risers, climbs from the yard to the front.
- At the top is the **front veranda**, a covered outdoor platform outside the main entrance.
- It is roofed by the **front balcony slab** above it, so it stays shaded and dry.
- It is the first space a visitor steps onto before entering the house.

### 2.4 Kitchen (Room 1)
- Closed off from the living spaces by a **half height wall with a door**. Light and conversation can pass over the wall, while cooking stays contained.
- The upper floor **open lounge** sits directly above it (see 3.1).

### 2.5 Staircase
- Sits **directly in front of the kitchen**, at the edge of the double height living hall.
- An **open staircase**: it rises inside the living hall itself, not in a closed stairwell, so you see it from the sitting room.
- A **square staircase**: the flights turn around a square plan with landings at the corners, rather than rising in one long straight run.
- It is the only link between the two floors. It arrives upstairs through an opening in the upper floor slab, beside the open lounge.

### 2.6 Bedroom corridor
- Begins **beside the kitchen** and leads away from the living hall into the private bedroom wing.
- **Bedroom 2** is on one side and **Bedroom 3** on the other.
- The **guest bedroom** closes the far end of the corridor.

### 2.7 Bedroom 2 (Room 2) and Bedroom 3 (Room 3)
- Two bedrooms facing each other across the corridor.
- The master bedroom sits directly above both of them (see 3.3).

### 2.8 Guest bedroom
- At the **end of the corridor**, inside the house.
- A shared room for visiting family and guests, reached only through the corridor.

### 2.9 Self contained Room 4
- Joins the corridor wing at a right angle, so together they form an **L shape** on plan.
- An **independent room**: it can be used without passing through the rest of the bedroom wing.
- The home office sits directly above it (see 3.4).

## 3. Upper floor

### 3.1 Open landing lounge
- At the top of the stairs, **directly above the kitchen**.
- A room with **no door**, open to the landing on one side, with a **window** for daylight and air.
- Its block walls rise in an **L** around the head of the stairs. The open side faces the landing.
- Suits a reading corner, a family lounge or a children's study area.

### 3.2 L shaped gallery
- An **internal walkway** shaped like an L, running along the edge of the double height living hall.
- It **overlooks the living hall** below, like a mezzanine. The open side is the edge of the upper floor slab, where it stops over the living hall.
- That edge needs a **balustrade**, a protective railing, along its full length (see section 9).
- It is the main circulation route upstairs:
  - from the stairs and the open lounge,
  - to the **master bedroom door**,
  - and out to the **front balcony**.

### 3.3 Master bedroom
- Sits **above Bedroom 2 and Bedroom 3**, the largest private room in the house.
- Its main door opens onto the **L shaped gallery**, facing the open void of the living hall.
- It connects internally to the **home office** (see 3.4).
- The **en suite bathroom** is at its far end (see 3.5).

### 3.4 Home office
- Sits **directly above Room 4**.
- A connecting room with **two doors**:
  - one into the **master bedroom**,
  - one onto the **front balcony**.
- You can reach work without crossing the shared gallery, and step outside for air without passing through the bedroom.

### 3.5 En suite bathroom
- At the **far end of the master bedroom**, away from the gallery.
- Private to the master suite, reached only through the bedroom.

### 3.6 Front balcony
- An outdoor platform at the front of the upper floor, **above the front veranda**.
- Reached from two places:
  - the **L shaped gallery**,
  - the **home office**.

## 5. How you move through the house

**Arriving:** entrance steps → front veranda → main entrance → open plan dining area → double height living hall.

**Ground floor, private wing:** living hall → bedroom corridor (beside the kitchen) → Bedroom 2 or Bedroom 3 → guest bedroom at the end. Room 4 is reached independently, at the corner of the L.

**Going upstairs:** living hall → square staircase (in front of the kitchen) → open landing lounge → L shaped gallery.

**Upper floor:**
- Gallery → master bedroom → en suite bathroom
- Gallery → front balcony
- Master bedroom → home office → front balcony

The front balcony and the home office each have two ways in. That makes the upper floor a **loop**: gallery, master bedroom, office, balcony, and back to the gallery.

## 7. Words used in this description

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Double height | A room with no floor above it, so the ceiling rises through both storeys |
| Void | The open air space above a double height room, seen from the upper floor |
| Gallery | An upper floor walkway that overlooks a double height space, with a railing on the open side |
| Open plan | Two uses, such as living and dining, in one space with no wall between them |
| Half height wall | A wall built to about waist or chest height, which divides a space without closing it off |
| Square staircase | A stair that turns around a square plan, with landings at the corners |
| En suite | A bathroom reached only from the bedroom it serves |
| Self contained room | A room you can use without passing through other private rooms |
| Veranda | A covered platform at ground level outside the entrance |
| Balcony | A platform on an upper floor, open to the outside, with a railing |

## 8. Points to confirm

The site photos answered two questions. Two remain:

1. Does the **guest bedroom** sit beside Room 4, as drawn in the sketch, or separately at the end of the corridor?
2. Does the L shaped gallery run along **one side or two sides** of the living hall void?

3. **Where are the ground floor bathroom and toilet?** The description has none, but Bedroom 2, Bedroom 3, the guest bedroom and visitors in the living hall all need one. The natural place is off the bedroom corridor. Room 4, if it is truly self contained, would also want its own shower room.
4. **What is the roof?** The 3D model shows a flat roof hidden behind a low parapet wall, which suits the white and black design. If the roof will be iron sheets or tiles, the model changes.

Answered by the photos:
- The staircase rises **inside the living hall**, not in an enclosed stairwell.
- The front veranda is **roofed by the balcony slab** above it.

## 9. What the site photos show

### From the road, before the slab was cast
- Timber props hold up the formwork for the **upper floor slab**, over the front of the house.
- Under it is the broad flight of **entrance steps** onto the raised plinth. The slab above them becomes the floor of the front balcony and the roof of the veranda.
- Behind, the block walls stand taller than the rest of the house. That tall section is the **double height living hall**.
- Tall steel cages stand at the corners. These are **column reinforcement**, waiting to be cast in concrete.

### On the upper floor slab, now
- New block walls are rising in an **L** beside the stair opening. They form the **open landing lounge** around the head of the stairs.
- The other upper floor walls are being built off the slab, along the edge of the living hall void. They are now about chest height.

### Looking from the slab towards the living hall
The view is from the **upper floor slab**, the concrete deck over the kitchen and bedroom wing.

### Things to plan now

1. **Guard the gallery edge.** Today the slab edge drops a full storey into the living hall with nothing to stop a fall. Fit a temporary barrier while building continues. Then plan a permanent balustrade, at least 1.0 m high (1.1 m is safer), with gaps no wider than 10 cm so a child cannot slip through. Glass, steel or timber all work. This edge will be seen from the whole sitting room, so it is a design feature as well as a safety one.
2. **Guard the stair opening and the stairs.** The opening in the slab needs a railing on its open sides, and the stairs need a handrail, especially where they run along the open side of the living hall.
3. **Light the tall room.** A double height living hall can feel dark at the bottom. High windows near the top of its tall walls, called **clerestory windows**, bring daylight deep into the room and let hot air escape. Plan them before the ring beam is cast, because the openings are much harder to add later.

4. **Check headroom over the stairs.** Part of the slab overhangs the upper flight. Anyone climbing needs at least **2.0 m** of clear height above every step, measured straight up to the underside of the slab. Check it with a tape now, while the slab opening can still be adjusted.
5. **Borrow light for the open lounge.** Its walls are being built along the edge of the living hall. An internal opening or window in that wall would let the lounge look down into the hall and share its high light. It would also make the upper floor feel connected to the sitting room below. This is easy to leave out while the blocks are going up, and costly to cut in later.
6. **Edge protection for the workers.** The photos show masons working at the slab edge above a full storey drop. A simple timber rail along the open edges protects them now, and the same line marks where the permanent balustrade will go.

These photos show only parts of the house, so treat the sketch in section 6 as approximate until it is checked against the plans.

## 10. Finishes and colour scheme

The house is **white, with grey as the quiet second colour and black for the fine details**. White carries the walls, grey carries the base and the floors, and black draws the lines.

| Element | Finish |
|---|---|
| External walls | Smooth white render |
| Plinth and entrance steps | Grey |
| Entrance bay | A full height grey panel around the main door, edged with thin black lines |
| Windows and glazed doors | Black frames, clear glass |
| Balcony, gallery and stair railings | Black, with upright bars and a black top rail |
| Parapet | White, finished with a thin black coping along the top |
| Front door and internal doors | Black |
| Internal walls and ceilings | White |
| Floors | Light grey tiles; grey outdoor tiles on the veranda and balcony |
| Kitchen | Black units, white worktops, grey splashback, black fridge and hood |
| Living hall | Grey sofa and armchairs, black coffee table, grey TV feature wall, a black ring chandelier hanging the full height of the hall |
| Bedrooms | Black beds and wardrobes, white bedding, grey throws |
| En suite | White bath, basin and WC, glass shower with a black frame, black vanity |
| Lights outside | Black wall lights beside the doors |
| Downpipes and gate | Black |
| Boundary wall | White, with a black coping and a black sliding gate |

**The rule that keeps it calm:** about 70 percent white, 20 percent grey, 10 percent black. If a room starts to feel heavy, it has too much black.
