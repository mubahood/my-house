# Living hall ceiling: six minimalist ideas

Six ceiling-focused visual studies for the same double-height living hall. Each is viewed from below and to the side so the chandelier's full hanging profile is clear; the frame stays tight on the ceiling and fixture, without showing the TV wall or furniture. The same crystal chandelier is used in every image while the ceiling detail changes.

## Shared rules

- Keep the centre of the ceiling plain and uncluttered. Moulding or shaped plaster stays at the perimeter and in the corners.
- Keep the centre of the ceiling plain apart from the chandelier. Moulding and black reveals stay at the perimeter and corners.
- Use black flush speaker grilles, black-trim recessed spots, and matte-black shadow lines as one coordinated detail.
- Use the supplied tall crystal cascade chandelier, with its champagne-gold frame, as the shared focal point. The chandelier is the only gold element.
- Keep the ceiling white plaster and the design restrained so the chandelier remains the centrepiece.
- Confirm ceiling heights, electrical positions, speaker wiring and the chandelier support with the architect/electrician before construction.

## Practical coordination

- Lay out spotlights for even light; use dimmable circuits and black-trim fittings to align with the reveals.
- Coordinate flush speaker grilles and their wiring with the audio system before closing the ceiling.
- Specify a warm-neutral dimmable LED for coves. Hide the strip from normal seated views and provide access to the driver.
- Confirm the chandelier is supported by the ceiling structure, especially in this double-height space.
- The visualisations are design studies, not construction drawings; have dimensions and fixing details checked against the final plan.
